Correcting an interest rate for inflation

The nominal rate is what the market quotes; the real rate is what the money buys. Subtracting inflation is a quick approximation; dividing (1 + nominal) by (1 + inflation) is exact. Questions 5(c) and 6(b).

Reading

The Fisher relationship. (1 + real) × (1 + inflation) = 1 + nominal. Lenders add an inflation premium to the real return they require; borrowers repay in money that buys less. The approximation is good when both rates are small, and drifts as they grow.
