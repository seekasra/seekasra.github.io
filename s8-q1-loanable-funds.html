Finance and the Economy · Seminar 8 · Macroeconomic factors

Less inventory, less borrowing, a lower interest rate

The interest rate is the price of borrowing. Firms that no longer need to finance large inventories demand fewer loanable funds, so the demand curve shifts left and the rate falls, which encourages other borrowers, such as firms investing in factories and equipment. Why it matters for interest rates. Anything that changes how much firms, households or governments want to borrow, or how much savers want to lend, moves these curves and so moves the interest rate, even before the central bank does anything.
